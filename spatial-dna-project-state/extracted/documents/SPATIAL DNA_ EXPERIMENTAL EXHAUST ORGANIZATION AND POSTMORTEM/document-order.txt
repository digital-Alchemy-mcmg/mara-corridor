2. Instructional Chronology and Intent
Mapping instructions to intent is a critical forensic step in identifying "Spark Drift"—the delta between the designer’s directive and the system's actual interpretation. By documenting these points of transition, we can see where the system successfully adhered to constraints and where it required manual intervention to prevent the collapse of complex data.
Instruction: Fracture the Single Mound
Designer’s Intent:  To force the semantic map to separate into distinct topographical islands (Operations, Software, Finance) rather than grouping all data as a single biographical block.
Spark’s Interpretation:  Initially collapsed all 44 atoms into a single entity; required a specific "Cluster by" directive (Functional Domain and Capability Class) to achieve the intended topographical "fracture."
Instruction: Zero Drift Mandate
Designer’s Intent:  To enforce a "Strategy Lock" and "No Drift" policy, specifically preventing the  hallucinated claim inflation  often used to artificially match job keywords.
Spark’s Interpretation:  Successfully implemented a "Conflict Preservation" rule, flagging contradictions as CONFLICTED rather than normalizing them to fit a clean narrative.This sequence of commands moved the experiment from a theoretical framework into a set of tangible, auditable artifacts.
